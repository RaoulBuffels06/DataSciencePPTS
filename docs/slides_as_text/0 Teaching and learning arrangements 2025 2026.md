## Slide 12
- Evaluation January

- 12

- Individual project: 20%
- Kickoff: during class in week 4
- Deadline: 16/11 23:59
- Based on chapters 2 + 3
- The student scrapes data from the web, researches the data and presents the results.
- As AI-based coding assistants can be used, the emphasis is on analysis and interpretation rather than correct syntax.
- Assessment is based on explanation during an oral exam, not on the code provided in the solution.
- Projects not submitted by the deadline will not be assessed.
- Copying or having copied is considered exam fraud.
- Be aware that your grades are retained for the 2nd chance exam in August

## Slide 13
- Evaluation January

- 13

- Digital exam on own laptop: 80 %
- The student analyses data by coding in Python and interprets the results.
- question 1:  Numpy & Matplotlib (30%)
- question 2:  Pandas (50%)
- The exam is open book and open internet.
- As AI-based coding assistants can be used, the emphasis is on analysis and interpretation rather than correct syntax.
- The exam is taken on your own laptop. You are responsible for working hardware and software.
- Communication between students is not allowed.
- Copying or having copied is considered exam fraud.

## Slide 14
- Evaluation January

- 14

- Realize that we are not just testing your programming skills!
- During the oral defense of your project, you should be able to thoroughly explain the programming choices you have made. It is not enough that your code works. The emphasis is on understanding the code and interpreting the results correctly.
- The same applies to the digital exam.  You solve 2 exercises, but besides correct code, your analytical skills and understanding of the statistical output received are graded.
